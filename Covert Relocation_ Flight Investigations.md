# **Intelligence Assessment: The Shadow Air-Removal Apparatus**

## **I. Executive Summary & Key Judgements**

This report provides a comprehensive assessment of a covert international human relocation apparatus operated by, and on behalf of, the United States government. The investigation reveals a complex, multi-layered system designed for the mass removal of individuals, leveraging a combination of privatized logistics, military assets, advanced surveillance technology, and a concurrent strategy to degrade legal and human rights frameworks. The findings are based on an analysis of government contracts, official agency documents, flight tracking data, corporate records, and investigative journalism.  
**Key Judgement 1:** The U.S. government operates a dual-track air removal system. This system synergizes the publicly acknowledged but opaque, privatized "ICE Air Operations" with deniable, off-the-books military airlifts. This dual structure enables the state to conduct mass human relocations with operational flexibility, including the transfer of individuals to extra-legal destinations where due process is effectively suspended.  
**Key Judgement 2:** A symbiotic ecosystem of politically connected private contractors has emerged as the primary logistical force for this apparatus. These entities, sustained by multi-billion-dollar, often non-competitive government contracts, provide a layer of plausible deniability for state actions. They profit directly from operations linked to systemic and well-documented human rights abuses, creating a powerful financial incentive for the continuation and expansion of the removal network.  
**Key Judgement 3:** Advanced data-fusion technology, principally Palantir Technologies' "ImmigrationOS" platform, serves as the central nervous system for the entire operation. This system enables the targeting, tracking, and logistical management of individuals on a mass scale by integrating vast, disparate datasets from public and private sources. This creates a seamless technological pipeline from domestic surveillance to physical apprehension and removal.  
**Key Judgement 4:** The physical removal operations are executed in parallel with a strategic legal and policy assault on established constitutional principles. This assault is designed to expand the pool of deportable individuals by dismantling norms such as birthright citizenship. The objective is to create a perpetually vulnerable and legally ambiguous underclass, thereby ensuring a continuous supply of targets for the removal apparatus.

## **II. The Dual-Track Air Removal Infrastructure**

Analysis indicates the U.S. government employs two parallel and complementary aviation networks to execute its human removal objectives. The first is a large-scale, privatized charter system operating under the official banner of ICE Air Operations. The second is a more clandestine military airlift capability, activated for surge capacity, political messaging, and missions requiring the unique, extra-legal reach of the Department of Defense. This dual structure allows for operational adaptability, enabling the state to normalize mass removals through the privatized channel while reserving military assets for operations that fall outside the bounds of standard legal and civil oversight.

### **2.1. ICE Air Operations (IAO): The Official Privatized Framework**

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officially designates its Air Operations (IAO) division as the primary entity for facilitating the transfer and removal of noncitizens. IAO supports all 25 Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO) field offices, coordinating removals via both commercial airlines and, more significantly, chartered flights. This network represents a fundamental strategic shift from a previous model run by the U.S. Marshals Service using government aircraft to what is now a "sprawling, semi-secret network of flights on privately-owned aircraft". This privatization outsources direct operational control, complicates accountability, and creates a lucrative market for a select group of contractors.  
IAO's operational tempo is significant. In Fiscal Year (FY) 2023, ERO conducted 142,580 removals to over 170 countries. In a concentrated period between June and September 2024, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) operated over 495 international repatriation flights. The reliance on the less transparent charter system is overwhelming; official reports indicate that charter flights outpace commercial flights for deportation by a ratio of approximately 20 to 1\.  
The operation is structured around five main hubs: Mesa, Arizona; Brownsville, Texas; San Antonio, Texas; Alexandria, Louisiana; and Miami, Florida. From these staging points, IAO can conduct missions worldwide. Flights are categorized based on their destination and risk profile:

* **Daily Scheduled Large Aircraft (DSLA):** The most common type, typically using Boeing 737 or MD-80 series aircraft for destinations in the Western Hemisphere.  
* **Long Range International Charters (LRIC):** Less frequent flights to more distant locations such as those in Africa or Asia.  
* **Special High-Risk Charters (SHRC):** These flights are used for individuals deemed security risks, those who have previously resisted deportation, or for missions requiring complex landing permissions. SHRC missions may use a range of aircraft, from small Gulfstream IV jets to large Boeing 777s.

The financial scale of this privatized network is immense. A daily scheduled charter flight costs an average of $8,577 per flight hour. The cost for a Special High-Risk Charter can range from $6,929 to $26,795 per flight hour, depending on the aircraft and mission requirements. This cost structure creates a multi-billion dollar revenue stream for the private companies that operate these flights.

### **2.2. Department of Defense Augmentation: The Military Airlift Vector**

Concurrent with the privatized IAO system, the executive branch has activated a military airlift capability for deportation. Citing a national emergency at the southern border, the Pentagon was directed to provide aircraft and personnel to fly migrants out of the country. This brought U.S. Air Force assets, specifically C-17 Globemaster III and C-130 Hercules cargo planes, directly into the removal process. The initial deployment involved four aircraft staged in El Paso, Texas, and San Diego, California, with approximately 100 airmen assigned to the mission.  
Investigative work by the University of Washington Center for Human Rights (UWCHR) identified Joint Base Lewis-McChord (JBLM) in Washington State as a critical hub for these military deportation flights. Between January 24 and February 13, 2025, a majority of these military-run flights were conducted by planes based at JBLM, identified by their distinctive green-striped tail flash markings.  
A specific case provides a clear link between a military asset and a deportation mission. A C-17 aircraft with tail number 09-9210 was documented as being stationed at JBLM on February 10, 2025\. Two days later, on February 12, this same aircraft was used to deport 119 individuals, predominantly of Asian nationalities, to Panama. Public flight history for tail number 09-9210 confirms its base of operations as Tacoma (McChord Field \- TCM), corroborating the UWCHR finding. The use of military transports for these missions is notably more expensive and less efficient than the civilian charters used by IAO. This suggests the motivation is not logistical but political: to project a "hard line" on immigration through the visible use of military force.  
Most alarmingly, this military vector is not limited to deportations to home countries or third-party nations. The investigation reveals that these same JBLM-based military planes have been used to transport detained immigrants to the U.S. naval base at Guantánamo Bay, Cuba. This action moves individuals outside of the U.S. domestic legal system and into a notorious legal "black box." ProPublica reports and an ACLU lawsuit describe detainees at Guantánamo as being held incommunicado, with family members and even attorneys denied access and information about their legal status or whereabouts. The transfer of immigration detainees to Guantánamo represents a severe escalation, placing individuals in a jurisdiction where constitutional protections and due process are effectively non-existent.  
The existence of this military track alongside the privatized IAO system creates a functionally tiered removal apparatus. The high-volume, continuous operations of IAO serve to normalize the process of mass deportation, creating a constant stream of activity that provides cover and a logistical baseline. The military airlift vector is then reserved for operations that serve a distinct purpose: political messaging through the use of force projection, surge capacity during crackdowns, and, most critically, the transfer of individuals to extra-legal jurisdictions like Guantánamo Bay, far from the reach of U.S. courts and public scrutiny.

## **III. The Contractor Ecosystem: Profit, Politics, and Plausible Deniability**

The execution of the air removal apparatus is dependent on a web of private corporations. These contractors, from prime air brokers to subcontractors and ground support firms, form an ecosystem fueled by lucrative government contracts and sustained by deep political connections. This structure not only provides the logistical capacity for mass removals but also insulates the government from direct accountability for the controversial and often abusive methods employed.

### **3.1. Prime Contractors: The Brokers of Removal**

At the apex of the contractor hierarchy are the prime brokers who arrange the charter flights directly with ICE.  
**CSI Aviation, Inc.:** A privately held company based in Albuquerque, New Mexico, CSI has emerged as one of ICE's top deportation flight contractors. Founded in 1979 by Allen Weh, a retired Marine Corps Colonel and prominent Republican donor, the company has deep political roots. CSI has been awarded over $2 billion in federal contracts, with a dramatic increase in recent years corresponding with heightened immigration enforcement. The company holds GSA Schedule Contract \#GS-33F-0025V for air charter services and is also a party to a $5.7 billion Department of Defense airlift contract, illustrating its deep integration with government operations. In a notable instance, ICE awarded CSI a no-bid contract for removal flights worth up to $128 million, which was later modified to increase capacity for a "surge" in deportations. Despite its central role in the deportation machinery, CSI's corporate website makes no mention of its work for ICE, a clear effort to manage public perception.  
**Classic Air Charter (CAC):** A key competitor to CSI, this Huntington, New York-based company has also held massive contracts for ICE air services. Its previous contract, running from 2017 to 2023, was worth nearly $885 million. A 2018 Government Accountability Office (GAO) document resolving a bid protest between the two firms reveals the colossal financial stakes involved. For a 2017 request for quotations, CAC submitted a bid of $646 million, while CSI bid $852.2 million. The political dimensions of this business are further highlighted by reports that CAC's president, Gwen Carson, made multiple donations to Trump-affiliated PACs.

### **3.2. The Subcontractor Web: The Wings of Removal**

The prime contractors subcontract the actual flight operations to a network of charter airlines, many of which also serve high-profile commercial clients, creating a stark contrast in their business portfolios.  
**GlobalX (Global Crossing Airlines):** This Miami-based airline has become a dominant subcontractor for CSI, operating a significant majority of ICE's removal flights in 2024\. The airline, which publicly promotes its services for professional sports teams and celebrity tours for artists like Lady Gaga and Bad Bunny, has a five-year contract with ICE that it expects will generate $65 million in annual revenue. GlobalX was the airline that operated flights to El Salvador in March 2025 that proceeded despite a federal judge issuing an order to turn the planes back more than 45 minutes before landing, raising questions of deliberate defiance of a court order. ProPublica interviews with current and former GlobalX flight attendants revealed they were often misled about the nature of their work, believing they would be flying VIPs, only to find themselves staffing deportation flights. These employees expressed grave safety concerns, noting a lack of training on how to evacuate a plane full of detainees shackled at the wrists and ankles.  
**Avelo Airlines:** A Texas-based budget carrier that, facing financial difficulties, signed a contract to operate three of its aircraft for deportation flights based out of Mesa, Arizona. In an internal memo, CEO Andrew Levy described the contract as "too valuable not to pursue" for the airline's financial stability. The decision has triggered significant public and political backlash, including customer boycotts and legislative threats in Connecticut, where Avelo has a major hub and receives state tax incentives.  
**iAero Airways (formerly Swift Air):** Before its bankruptcy in 2023, iAero was a cornerstone of the deportation network. Bankruptcy filings revealed the company operated nearly 60 deportation flights per month, at one point earning approximately $4 million per week from this business. Like GlobalX, iAero also flew for teams in the NHL, NBA, and MLS, showcasing the dual nature of this industry. Its bankruptcy demonstrates both the immense profitability and the potential volatility of relying on government enforcement contracts.  
**Other Carriers:** The network is extensive and includes **World Atlantic** and **Omni Air International**, a subsidiary of Air Transport Services Group which also provides cargo aircraft for Amazon and DHL. Smaller jets operated by companies like **Gryphon** are used for long-distance, special high-risk charters to destinations in Africa, the Pacific, and Europe.

## **IV. Operational Tactics for Covert Movement & Evasion**

The air removal apparatus employs a range of operational tactics designed to obscure its movements and evade oversight from watchdog groups and the public. These methods include manipulating aviation signals, exploiting known surveillance gaps, and operating under the cover of darkness.

### **4.1. Aviation Signal Evasion and Identity Obfuscation**

A primary method of maintaining secrecy involves the manipulation of aircraft identification and tracking signals broadcast via the Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast (ADS-B) system.  
**Call Sign Manipulation:** For years, a key tool for independent researchers tracking deportation flights was the ICAO-assigned radiotelephony call sign prefix "RPN," an abbreviation for "Repatriate". Aviation forum discussions among air traffic control monitors confirm that the "Repatriate" call sign is used specifically when flights have detainees on board, distinguishing them from "Justice" call signs used for other U.S. Marshals or government flights. However, following the publication of UWCHR's "Hidden in Plain Sight" report, which detailed this tracking method, ICE reportedly changed its flight numbering system, abandoning the consistent use of the "RPN" prefix to make its flights more difficult to identify and track. Analysis of flight data revealed that certain carriers, such as Omni Air International, already did not use the "RPN" prefix for their SHRC flights, making them indistinguishable from other private charters. This demonstrates a conscious effort to blend into regular air traffic and avoid scrutiny.  
**Transponder Deactivation and Signal Loss:** Multiple online forums for aviation enthusiasts and flight tracking hobbyists contain discussions of aircraft disappearing from public tracking sites, particularly over water or in sensitive regions. While intermittent signal loss can be attributed to poor ADS-B receiver coverage, users note that these disappearances are not always consistent for all aircraft in the same airspace, raising suspicion of deliberate transponder deactivation. One user on a flight radar forum explicitly states that military aircraft "normally turn their transponder off before they get there". Another thread from 2024 documents a mass deactivation of transponders on U.S. military aircraft in a specific region. These anecdotal reports, combined with the operational need for secrecy, strongly suggest that disabling transponders is a tactic used to create "dark" segments of a flight path, particularly over areas like the Gulf of Mexico and the Caribbean Sea, which serve as corridors to Central America and Guantánamo Bay.  
**Use of Privacy ICAO Address (PIA):** The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) operates a program that allows aircraft owners to request a Privacy ICAO Address (PIA). This program replaces the aircraft's permanent and publicly registered 24-bit ICAO address with a temporary, anonymous one. While the aircraft remains fully visible to FAA air traffic control for safety and separation, its identity is masked from third-party ADS-B receivers, such as those used by public flight tracking websites and watchdog groups. While intended for legitimate privacy reasons, this system provides a ready-made, FAA-sanctioned mechanism for any operator, including government contractors, to obscure their identity. The FAA notes that improper use, such as using a PIA assigned to another aircraft or changing it mid-flight, is prohibited, but such violations would be nearly impossible for an outside observer to detect.

### **4.2. Exploitation of Surveillance Gaps**

The physical infrastructure of air traffic surveillance contains inherent weaknesses that can be exploited to conduct flights with a reduced signature.  
**ADS-B "Dark Zones":** The global ADS-B network relies heavily on a web of ground-based receivers. These receivers are limited by the line-of-sight principle, meaning their range can be impeded by terrain like mountains or simply by the curvature of the Earth over vast bodies of water. This creates significant surveillance gaps, or "dark zones," over oceans, remote land areas, and mountainous regions. The Gulf of Mexico, the Caribbean Sea, and large parts of Central and South America are known areas of weaker ground-based coverage. Flights can be routed through these corridors to minimize their visibility on public tracking platforms that rely on this ground network.  
**Inconsistent Mandates in Central America:** The adoption of ADS-B technology is not uniform. As of late 2024, Guatemala and El Salvador were set to mandate ADS-B Out for aircraft in their airspace. However, neighboring countries in the Central American Flight Information Region (FIR), including Belize, Costa Rica, Honduras, and Nicaragua, had not yet published similar mandates. This patchwork of surveillance creates seams and corridors where aircraft can operate under older, less precise radar tracking or without any public visibility at all, allowing for strategic routing to avoid detection.  
**Satellite-Based ADS-B as a Countermeasure:** The existence of these vulnerabilities has spurred the development of countermeasures. Companies like Aireon are deploying satellite constellations equipped with ADS-B receivers to close these terrestrial coverage gaps and provide 100% global surveillance. This technology not only tracks flights over oceans and remote areas but can also be used to identify GNSS anomalies (jamming or spoofing) by independently calculating an aircraft's position via multilateration (MLAT). The very development of this technology underscores that the vulnerabilities it is designed to fix—coverage gaps and the potential for location spoofing—are recognized and significant operational concerns.

## **V. The Palantir Surveillance and Targeting Engine**

The logistical complexity of identifying, tracking, apprehending, and removing hundreds of thousands of individuals requires a powerful technological backbone. Analysis reveals that this function is fulfilled by Palantir Technologies, a data analytics firm with deep, long-standing ties to U.S. intelligence and law enforcement agencies. The relationship between ICE and Palantir transcends a typical client-vendor dynamic; Palantir has effectively architected the central nervous system of the modern removal apparatus, blurring the line between a private tech company and a core function of the state.

### **5.1. "ImmigrationOS": The Logistical Backbone of Deportation**

Palantir, a company co-founded by billionaire tech investor and political activist Peter Thiel, has been a contractor for ICE for over a decade. In 2025, this partnership culminated in a $30 million no-bid contract for Palantir to build a new surveillance platform known as "ImmigrationOS". This system is not a standalone product but rather an advanced, integrated layer built directly on top of Palantir's existing Investigative Case Management (ICM) system. The ICM system is already considered "mission critical" by ICE and serves as the primary software environment for its Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) directorate, used to manage case files, track targets, and exchange intelligence.  
The stated purpose of ImmigrationOS is to dramatically accelerate and streamline the deportation process. Leaked internal communications and contract documents reveal its core functions :

* **Targeting and Identification:** To streamline the identification and apprehension of individuals prioritized for removal, including those with criminal records, alleged gang members, and visa overstays. Leaked messages show its role includes "producing leads for law enforcement to find people to deport".  
* **Location and Tracking:** To find the "physical location of people who are marked for deportation". The system provides "near real-time visibility" into the movements of migrants.  
* **Logistical Management:** To manage and track the logistics of the entire mass deportation effort, from apprehension to removal, including monitoring those who "self-deport".

To achieve this, ImmigrationOS is designed as a massive data-fusion engine. It consolidates information from a vast array of disparate government and private-sector databases, creating what has been described as a "master database" for surveillance. The scope of this data collection is staggering; by one 2022 estimate, ICE was able to access the home addresses of three out of four U.S. adults, regardless of their citizenship status. This vast, interoperable network gives enforcement agents an unprecedented ability to build profiles, locate targets, and execute arrests on a mass scale.

### **5.2. The Thiel-Miller Political-Technical Axis**

The selection of Palantir to build this critical infrastructure was not the result of a standard competitive procurement process. Instead, it appears to be the product of a powerful political-technical alignment between key administration figures and the company's leadership.  
Palantir's co-founder and chairman, Peter Thiel, is a prominent and influential ally of the Trump administration. More directly, Stephen Miller, a senior White House advisor and the primary architect of the administration's most hardline immigration policies, has a significant personal financial stake in the company. Financial disclosures reveal Miller holds between $100,001 and $250,000 in Palantir stock. This creates a direct and deeply problematic feedback loop: Miller is instrumental in creating the policies that require a mass deportation infrastructure, and his personal wealth increases as the company contracted to build that infrastructure profits.  
The no-bid nature of the $30 million ImmigrationOS contract was justified by ICE on the grounds that only Palantir possessed the "deep institutional knowledge of ICE operations" and was the only vendor capable of delivering the system on an accelerated timeline. This justification, viewed in the context of the political connections, suggests the procurement process was a formality, with Palantir being the preordained choice. The decision came after DHS Secretary Kristi Noem toured a "new high-tech hub for monitoring immigrants" with Palantir executives.  
Further evidence of this alignment comes from the reported influence of the White House's Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), an initiative associated with Elon Musk that aims to "centralize data all across government". This department reportedly employed Stephen Miller's wife, Katie Miller, as well as at least three former Palantir employees. This indicates a concerted effort by a politically-aligned cohort of tech executives and government officials to re-engineer federal enforcement through data centralization, with Palantir as the primary technological vehicle.

### **5.3. Data, Surveillance, and Counter-Surveillance**

The opacity of the ICE-Palantir system has made external oversight nearly impossible, necessitating novel forms of "counter-surveillance" by researchers and activists.  
The most significant breakthrough in understanding the scale of the removal apparatus came from the University of Washington Center for Human Rights (UWCHR). Using the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA), UWCHR obtained a massive dataset from ICE's own Alien Repatriation Tracking System (ARTS). The dataset, containing over 1.7 million passenger records on nearly 15,000 flights from October 2010 to December 2018, provided the first-ever public, data-driven view into the machinery of ICE Air.  
Crucially, UWCHR made its data and analysis methods publicly available through a GitHub repository. This repository includes the cleaned datasets, Jupyter notebooks used for analysis, and detailed documentation on the data structure and cleaning process. This act of radical transparency allows other researchers to verify their findings and conduct their own investigations, representing a key check on the state's secret operations by using the state's own data against it.  
Complementing this deep historical analysis is the near real-time monitoring conducted by activist groups like **Witness at the Border**. Led by researcher Tom Cartwright, these activists use publicly available commercial flight tracking applications like FlightAware to identify and report on deportation flights as they happen. This grassroots effort provides an ongoing, tactical-level view of the network's activities, tracking flight volumes and identifying the specific airlines involved.  
The combination of these factors—Palantir's "mission critical" role, its state of technological incumbency, the direct financial stake of the policy's architect, and the system's explicit purpose of generating leads for physical apprehension—indicates that Palantir is functioning as a privatized, for-profit, and largely unaccountable extension of the state's enforcement power. The line between government agency and core technology provider has been functionally erased, leaving the counter-surveillance efforts of academics and activists as one of the only remaining forms of oversight.

## **VI. Human Rights and Legal Framework Degradation**

The physical removal of individuals is the end point of a process characterized by systemic dehumanization and violence. This process is mirrored by a concurrent legal and policy strategy aimed at stripping individuals of fundamental rights, manipulating them into forfeiting legal options, and fundamentally altering the definition of citizenship to expand the deportable population. The physical and legal violence are two facets of a single, comprehensive strategy to maximize removals with minimal accountability.

### **6.1. Case Studies in In-Transit Abuse and Negligence**

The conditions aboard deportation flights are frequently abusive and unsafe, a direct result of a system that treats human beings as cargo.  
**The 2017 Somali Flight:** A CSI-contracted charter flight intended to deport 92 Somali nationals became a case study in extreme abuse and negligence. During a stopover in Senegal, the plane was grounded on the tarmac for approximately 23 hours. For the duration of the nearly two-day ordeal, the 92 detainees were kept in full restraints—handcuffs, waist chains, and leg shackles. According to a subsequent lawsuit, they were denied access to bathrooms, forcing some to urinate on themselves as toilets overflowed with human waste. Their requests for relief from painfully tight shackles were ignored, and those who protested were allegedly met with violence from ICE agents, including being kicked, struck, dragged, and immobilized in full-body restraints. The incident, which ended with the flight being recalled to the U.S., highlights a complete disregard for basic human dignity and safety.  
**The 2022 Camilo Rincon Assault:** On a December 2022 flight from Texas to Colombia, a detainee named Camilo Alexis Rincon, who was already in full restraints, was allegedly shoved and hit by guards from the security contractor Akima. An investigation by the DHS Office for Civil Rights and Civil Liberties (CRCL) found "sufficient evidence" to substantiate the claim of excessive force. The onboard nurse's report documented an "altercation," and Rincon was later diagnosed with contusions to his face, chest, and back. Critically, ICE failed to report the incident, and there was no video surveillance on the flight. CRCL recommended that ICE use body-worn cameras and retrain Akima guards, but the lack of transparency and initial reporting points to a culture of impunity.  
**Systemic Unsafe Conditions:** These are not isolated incidents but rather symptoms of a systemic problem. Flight attendants working on GlobalX deportation charters reported receiving no training on how to safely evacuate a plane full of shackled detainees in an emergency, a scenario they feared would inevitably end in disaster. Crew members were given strict rules to dehumanize the passengers: do not talk to them, do not feed them, do not make eye contact, and do not move about the cabin without a guard escort. Other documented safety failures include a CSI-contracted plane filling with smoke and fumes mid-flight with 129 detainees on board, and a landing gear failure on another flight that required an emergency evacuation.

### **6.2. The Strategic Assault on Birthright Citizenship**

Parallel to the physical operations, a legal and political campaign seeks to fundamentally alter U.S. citizenship law to create a larger pool of non-citizens vulnerable to the removal apparatus.  
The cornerstone of this campaign is the effort to end birthright citizenship as guaranteed by the Citizenship Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment. A January 20, 2025, Executive Order titled "Protecting the Meaning and Value of American Citizenship" purports to deny citizenship to children born in the U.S. if their parents are undocumented or hold a temporary status (e.g., student or tourist visa). This executive action is mirrored by proposed legislation, the Birthright Citizenship Act of 2025 (S. 304 / H.R. 569).  
This policy directly contradicts over a century of established legal precedent, most notably the 1898 Supreme Court case *United States v. Wong Kim Ark*, which affirmed that the Fourteenth Amendment grants citizenship to nearly all individuals born on U.S. soil. The executive order was immediately challenged in court and blocked by multiple federal judges as unconstitutional. However, the legal battle is ongoing and was expected to be decided by the Supreme Court, which could fundamentally reshape American citizenship. A June 2025 ruling cleared the way for the order to take effect in certain states, indicating a fractured and perilous legal landscape.  
The intended consequence of this policy is the creation of a massive, hereditary underclass. An analysis by the Migration Policy Institute projected that ending birthright citizenship for children of undocumented parents would add 4.7 million people to the undocumented population by 2050\. These individuals would exist in a state of legal limbo, lacking citizenship in the U.S. and often unable to claim citizenship in their parents' countries of origin, rendering them stateless. This pre-emptive stripping of legal personhood at birth dramatically and permanently expands the population subject to the surveillance and removal powers of the state.

### **6.3. Legally Coercive Removal Programs: The "Self-Deportation" Ruse**

For individuals already in the country without status, the administration has deployed programs that use legal manipulation to achieve removals. "Project Homecoming" is a "self-deportation" initiative that offers undocumented immigrants a free flight to their home country and a $1,000 stipend if they agree to leave voluntarily using the government's CBP Home mobile app.  
This program is framed as a humanitarian and empowering choice. However, immigration law experts have described it as a "deeply misleading and unethical trick". The program's marketing materials and the app itself fail to disclose a critical legal consequence: under U.S. immigration law, an individual who has been unlawfully present in the country for more than a year and then departs is automatically barred from re-entering the U.S. for ten years. This "re-entry bar" is statutory, meaning it was set by Congress, and cannot be waived by DHS through a mobile app. Individuals who accept the "self-deportation" offer unwittingly trigger this harsh penalty, forfeiting any potential future pathways to legal status that might have been available to them through a formal court process, such as a grant of "voluntary departure" from an immigration judge.  
The program's "voluntary" nature is rendered meaningless by the coercive environment in which it is offered. It is promoted alongside public threats of mass raids, daily fines, arrest, and permanent bars on re-entry for those who do not comply. This creates a climate of fear that pressures individuals into accepting a deceptive offer that severely damages their long-term legal standing. It is a form of legal entrapment designed to circumvent due process and achieve removal quotas.

## **VII. Intelligence Gaps and Avenues for Further Investigation**

While this report synthesizes a significant body of evidence, critical intelligence gaps remain. Closing these gaps is essential for a complete understanding of the removal apparatus and for developing effective accountability strategies. Further investigation should be prioritized in the following areas:

* **Aviation Operations Protocols:** The precise protocols governing the deactivation of ADS-B transponders on military and contractor flights are unknown. Obtaining internal FAA, DoD, or contractor standard operating procedures, or securing testimony from air traffic controllers or pilots involved in these flights, is critical. Leaked FAA logs, as have appeared in other contexts, could provide direct evidence of these practices.  
* **Corporate Structures and Ownership:** The full corporate ownership structures of the key air charter subcontractors—including iAero (prior to bankruptcy), World Atlantic, GlobalX, and others—remain partially obscured. While some executive information is public, deep-dive corporate registration searches, particularly in jurisdictions known for corporate secrecy like Delaware, are required to map out parent companies, subsidiaries, and potential shell corporations used to mask ownership and financial flows.  
* **Palantir's Private Data Sources:** The full list of private-sector data brokers and sources that feed into Palantir's ICM and ImmigrationOS platforms is a critical intelligence gap. Identifying these sources would reveal the true extent of the surveillance network's reach into commercial and public life, exposing how data collected for mundane purposes is weaponized for immigration enforcement.  
* **Military-Civilian Transfer Protocols:** The specific procedures for handing off detainees from ICE custody to Department of Defense custody for flights to extra-legal destinations like Guantánamo Bay are unknown. Understanding this chain of custody—including which agency has operational command, what legal authorities are cited, and what documentation is created—is vital for assessing the legality of these black-site transfers.  
* **Distinguishing "Ghost Flights":** The term "ghost flight" is used in aviation circles to describe commercially non-viable, near-empty flights operated solely to retain valuable airport slots. It is essential to distinguish this known, wasteful practice from the clandestine, unlisted flights that are the focus of this investigation. Further analysis is needed to determine if the former is ever used as a cover for the latter—for example, by filing a flight plan for a "ghost flight" that is then used for a covert transfer.  
* **Flight Pattern Spoofing and Detection:** The query referenced the possibility of flight pattern spoofing. While no direct evidence of this tactic was found, it remains a plausible threat vector. Further technical investigation should focus on the capabilities of systems designed to detect such anomalies. The technology used by companies like Aireon to independently verify aircraft position via satellite-based multilateration could offer insights into how spoofing or GNSS jamming can be identified and countered.  
* **Leaked Technical Artifacts:** The UWCHR GitHub repository provides a powerful model for analyzing official data. A similar effort is needed to acquire and analyze technical artifacts, such as leaked source code for flight planning software, internal Palantir system documentation, or tools related to ADS-B signal manipulation. Such artifacts would provide incontrovertible proof of the methods and capabilities of the removal apparatus.
